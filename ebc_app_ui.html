1 Choose videos

What each recording is for, and what makes one usable
RoleWhat it contains
Conditioning chapterPaired CS–US trials plus the CS-only probe that closes each block. Chapters run on one continuous clock, so their order matters.
ExtinctionCS alone after conditioning. Scored against the learned US window.
CS-only baselineCS alone, outside conditioning. Gives the false-positive rate for the CR window.
US-only baselineUS alone. No CS exists, so trials are anchored on the US automatically.

The one thing that breaks a recording is a CS LED with no contrast left. The stimulator box is wooden and warm-coloured, so as room light dims or warms, the box itself becomes as “yellow” as the CS LED. The resting level climbs toward the lit level, the detector ends up thresholding noise, and the trials are lost — two of one participant’s four conditioning recordings failed exactly this way.

When filming: keep the light on the box constant across all recordings of a session, avoid letting daylight fall on it, and keep the box in shot and in focus. Face roughly frontal, camera fixed. A camera re-aimed between recordings is fine; re-aimed within one is detected and flagged.

Nothing needs to be perfect — the app checks every LED itself and tells you what it found. Position of the face and of the LEDs is discovered per recording, so participants may sit differently and the box may be moved between sessions.

Nothing is uploaded and nothing is copied. The recordings are read where they sit, so a 4 GB file costs no extra disk and never leaves this computer.

Detection tolerances and the trial window

These decide which LED pulses count as stimuli and how much of each trial is tracked. The defaults suit almost every session; the trial window in particular follows the protocol on its own, and only needs setting by hand if you want more of the trial than the stimuli themselves call for.

SettingWhat it does
Minimum gap between trialsTwo CS onsets closer together than this cannot both be trials, so a flickering LED does not become a trial of its own.
Startle cut-off (fallback)Only used when the study has no US-only recording. With one, the reflex latency is measured from it instead — the mean of the unconditioned blink onsets minus 1.5 SD — and a blink counts as a CR from that long after the CS to that long after the US, because nothing can react to a stimulus sooner than the reflex itself takes. Without one, this number is the lower edge and the US onset is the upper one.
Duration tolerancesHow far a measured pulse may sit from the nominal duration and still be accepted. Every rejected pulse is still listed in stimulus_events.csv.
Trial windowHow much video is tracked around each trial. Left blank, the window after CS onset is worked out from the protocol — a trace design gets a longer one automatically, so the US and the response to it are inside it.
What the protocol is for, and when to change it

The protocol is used as a test, never as an instruction. Trials are recovered from what the LEDs actually did, and the result is then compared against the structure set above. Nothing is renumbered to make it fit.

So if the trial count comes out wrong, do not adjust these numbers to match. A disagreement is a finding about the recording — almost always a CS LED that could not be read — and the app will tell you which recording and why.

Do change them when the session really was run differently. The app does not require the US to fall inside the CS. Put the US onset at or after the CS duration and the study becomes a trace protocol: the diagram shows the gap, trials are paired across it, the trial window and the CR window stretch to cover it, and the figures and workbooks describe it as a trace design rather than a delay one.

Whatever is set here is written into the study file beside the results, so a number that reaches a paper can be traced back to the run that produced it. The last protocol actually used is remembered, so a lab that has moved to a different design does not retype it for every participant.

A full session of four 4 GB recordings takes roughly an hour. Finished work is cached, so re-running skips anything already done — only new or changed recordings cost time. It is safe to leave this running and come back; closing the black command window is what stops it.

2. Read the trial count as a result, not a formality. 93 found / 100 expected is a finding. 41 found / 100 is a detection failure to diagnose — not a participant who missed trials.

3. Check the provenance columns in the workbook’s Paired trials sheet: CS timing source says whether each CS onset was measured from the LED or inferred from the US; Block boundary from says whether each block was closed by an observed probe or by counting. A counted boundary is an assumption from the protocol; a probe is an observation.

EBC Analyzer — eyeblink conditioning, delay or trace, scored from video by the same pipeline every time.

Method
Both stimulus LEDs are read at full frame rate and every pulse detected; eyelid aperture is tracked from MediaPipe FaceMesh and scored against one pooled closure scale. Trials are recovered from what was measured, and the protocol is used to check that recovery, never to assert it.
