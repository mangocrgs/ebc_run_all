1 Choose videos

Tick the recordings to analyse. Videos are read where they sit — nothing is copied or uploaded, so a 4 GB file costs no extra disk. Conditioning chapters are stitched into one timeline in the order shown; use ▲▼ if the recording order differs.

What each recording is for, and what makes one usable
RoleWhat it contains
Conditioning chapterPaired CS–US trials plus the CS-only probe that closes each block. Chapters run on one continuous clock, so their order matters.
ExtinctionCS alone after conditioning. Scored against the learned US window.
CS-only baselineCS alone, outside conditioning. Gives the false-positive rate for the CR window.
US-only baselineUS alone. No CS exists, so trials are anchored on the US automatically.

The one thing that breaks a recording is a CS LED with no contrast left. The stimulator box is wooden and warm-coloured, so as room light dims or warms, the box itself becomes as “yellow” as the CS LED. The resting level climbs toward the lit level, the detector ends up thresholding noise, and the trials are lost — two of one participant’s four conditioning recordings failed exactly this way.

When filming: keep the light on the box constant across all recordings of a session, avoid letting daylight fall on it, and keep the box in shot and in focus. Face roughly frontal, camera fixed. A camera re-aimed between recordings is fine; re-aimed within one is detected and flagged.

Nothing needs to be perfect — the app checks every LED itself and tells you what it found. Position of the face and of the LEDs is discovered per recording, so participants may sit differently and the box may be moved between sessions.

2 Protocol

These numbers should not normally be changed

Every session runs the same experiment, and these are already it: CS 400 ms, US 50 ms co-terminating with it, so the US begins at 350 ms — and 9 paired trials + 1 CS-only probe, ten times over (100 trials).

The protocol is used as a test, never as an instruction. Trials are recovered from what the LEDs actually did, and the result is then compared against the structure above. Nothing is renumbered to make it fit.

So if the trial count comes out wrong, do not adjust these numbers to match. A disagreement is a finding about the recording — almost always a CS LED that could not be read — and the app will tell you which recording and why. Change these only to record a genuine deviation in how the session was actually run.

CS and US co-terminate: US onset + US duration should equal CS duration. The block structure is detected from the recording, not assumed.

A full session of four 4 GB recordings takes roughly an hour. Finished work is cached, so re-running skips anything already done — only new or changed recordings cost time. It is safe to leave this running and come back.

2. Read the trial count as a result, not a formality. 93 found / 100 expected is a finding. 41 found / 100 is a detection failure to diagnose — not a participant who missed trials.

3. Check the provenance columns in the workbook’s Paired trials sheet: CS timing source says whether each CS onset was measured from the LED or inferred from the US; Block boundary from says whether each block was closed by an observed probe or by counting. A counted boundary is an assumption from the protocol; a probe is an observation.

4. Filter quality = clean for the strictest subset. Flagged trials are kept in the tables rather than silently dropped, and shown in italics.
